# Enchanting

The enchanting table opens the **Catalogue**. There are no random offers, no lapis, and no rerolling.

## Building the room

The table reads the same fifteen positions vanilla always used: two rings of blocks around it, with a clear path so nothing blocks the connection.

What sits in those positions matters now.

**Chiseled bookshelves** are your spellbook. Any enchanted book stored in one becomes selectable in the Catalogue. A chiseled bookshelf holds six books, so a single shelf can teach six enchantments.

**Regular bookshelves** are a discount. Each one cuts reagent cost by 3.33%, capped at fifteen shelves for 50% off.

A good room is a mix: chiseled shelves for the enchantments you actually want, regular shelves in the remaining slots for the discount.

::note
Chiseled bookshelf contents are synced to every player, so the Catalogue works correctly in multiplayer and the hover labels show what is inside without opening the shelf.
::

## Using the Catalogue

The screen has three slots and a list.

1. **Item slot.** Put in the thing you want to enchant.
2. **Reagent slot.** Put in the material the enchantment demands.
3. **The list.** Each row is one enchantment, with a clickable Roman numeral per level.

Row colours tell you where you stand: **green** means the item already has that level, **bright** means you can afford it, **dimmed** means you cannot.

Hovering a level shows the full cost: reagent count, experience levels, and how many slots it will take.

**Quantity** depends on the enchantment. Fortune III costs 36 Emeralds, Mending four Ghast Tears, Knockback II four Pistons. The bookshelf discount cuts that by up to half, rounding up. Upgrading an existing enchantment charges only the difference, so stepping up one level at a time costs the same as going straight to the top. The [full reagent table](https://github.com/Fixed-By-Design/enchantment-overhaul/blob/dev/docs/Enchantment-List.md#reagent-table) lists every price.

**Experience** is 2, 4, 7 or 10 levels for I through IV, and 10 for anything above. Mending is a flat 8. Upgrades pay the delta.

## Slots

Every item has a budget. Enchantments cost slots equal to their level.

| Slots | Items |
| :-- | :-- |
| 3 | Wood, stone, copper, leather, bow, fishing rod, shield |
| 4 | Iron, chainmail, crossbow |
| 5 | Diamond, netherite, elytra, turtle helmet, mace, trident |
| 6 | All gold gear |

Mending always costs 3. Curses cost 0 and each one grants a bonus slot.

The pip bar at the bottom of the Catalogue shows the budget: filled pips are used, blinking pips are what you are about to spend.

## Grindstone and anvil

The grindstone strips every enchantment, curses included. The item keeps its whole slot budget, so it can be enchanted again right away.

The anvil repairs instead of combining. Put the item in with its repair material: each one restores a quarter of the durability, and the whole repair costs one level. Tools vanilla could only fix by combining two copies take a material too:

| Tool | Repair material |
| :-- | :-- |
| Bow, crossbow, fishing rod | String |
| Trident | Prismarine Shard |
| Shears | Iron Ingot |
| Flint and Steel | Flint |
| Carrot on a Stick | Carrot |
| Warped Fungus on a Stick | Warped Fungus |
| Brush | Feather |

## Getting books in the first place

Enchanted books are found, not made.

- **Structure chests** across the world each carry a themed pool. Ancient cities hold Veil, Wraith, Swift Sneak and Curse of Binding. Nether fortresses hold Fire Aspect, Flame, Venom and Looting. End city treasure is where Mending lives.
- **Villager trades.** Librarians sell Feather Falling and Knockback at tier 1. Clerics sell Frost Walker and Luck of the Sea. Weaponsmiths sell Step-Up.
- **The stronghold library** has been rebuilt around four chiseled bookshelves holding one book each.
